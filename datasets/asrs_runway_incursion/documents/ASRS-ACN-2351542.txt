Synopsis:
PA-32 pilot reported misinterpreting a clearance that resulted in a runway incursion and critical ground conflict.

Narrative:
While holding short of Runway XXR at Taxiway 1 at ZZZ Airport; I received a transmission that included flight following information (remain clear of Class B airspace; a departure frequency; and a discrete transponder code). At the time; I believed I was on the appropriate frequency for departure. Due to the high traffic volume; extended delay prior to taxi; and my position at the hold short line ready for departure; I misinterpreted this transmission and believed it was a takeoff clearance.I read back: 'cleared for takeoff Runway XXR; remain clear of the ZZZ1 Class Bravo; departure frequency; and squawk code.' The controller responded with 'readback correct.' I interpreted this as confirmation and believed it was a takeoff clearance. Believing I was cleared; I visually checked final approach and did not observe any traffic on final; and observed a Cirrus holding short at Taxiway 2 (full length); which I believed was the aircraft I had heard waiting for an IFR clearance to ZZZ2. I then entered Runway XXR from Taxiway 1. An aircraft was still on the runway but in the process of exiting; and after it cleared the runway; I started my takeoff roll.Sometime during the takeoff roll; I heard 'monitor tower;' which led me to recognize that I may not have been on the correct frequency or had not received a valid takeoff clearance. Around liftoff; I attempted to contact tower on the assigned frequency (XXX.X) but did not receive a response and then switched to the other tower frequency (XXX.XX) to establish communication. During this time; I heard another aircraft execute a go-around. I adjusted my climb profile by shallowing the climb to reduce the risk of conflict but still maintain a safe distance above the ground.The problem arose from a misinterpretation of flight following information as a takeoff clearance. I believe some of the contributing factors included high workload due to traffic volume; frequency congestion; extended ground delay leading to expectation bias; and the similarity in cadence between the transmission and a takeoff clearance. Receiving the flight following information while holding short of the runway; in a position normally associated with an imminent departure clearance; likely increased expectation bias and contributed to the misinterpretation. Additionally; in busy environments it is common for flight following to be delayed or unavailable; and because no earlier indication was given that flight following would be issued later; I did not anticipate receiving it at that point; which likely contributed to the confusion. My perception that I was on the appropriate frequency and ready for departure influenced my judgment and decision-making. The issue was discovered when I heard 'monitor tower' during the takeoff roll; which prompted immediate recognition of a discrepancy.Corrective actions included attempting to establish communication with tower; switching frequencies when necessary; and modifying my climb profile to maintain safe separation from other aircraft. No further conflict developed.To prevent recurrence; I will ensure I'm on the correct frequency prior to departure and will positively verify that an explicit takeoff clearance has been received from tower before entering the runway. I will also deliberately pause at the hold short line; particularly in high workload environments; to confirm clearance and reduce the effects of expectation bias.
