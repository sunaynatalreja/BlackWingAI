Synopsis:
A319 Captain reported executing a go-around to avoid a traffic conflict with an aircraft on the runway; they then experienced a flap overspeed and altitude deviation.

Narrative:
During this flight I was the Captain and Pilot Monitoring. On final approach to runway XX at ZZZ the departing aircraft on the runway began their takeoff roll; momentarily stopped; then began rolling again.  Tower cancelled their takeoff clearance and directed us to go around. The First Officer (PF) had disconnected the autopilot in the descent at 1000ft AGL and was hand flying the aircraft during the approach and go around.  The FO immediately initiated a go around at 400ft AGL as directed. All initial go around procedures were executed and call outs made. The aircraft was established in a climb; gear up; flaps 3.The aircraft had established a very high rate of climb and we were on the way to the published missed approach altitude of 3000ft. At 1000ft MSL the PF moved the thrust levers to the Climb detent. Also; at 1000ft MSL tower switched us to departure frequency. Immediately upon establishing radio contact with departure they told us to maintain 2000ft. Given our very high rate of climb; both of us immediately focused on altitude awareness and the need to level at 2000ft. Both of us reached for the altitude set knob to put 2000ft in the window. Eventually; it was set; and 200kts was set in the speed window. The PF began to lower the nose and rate of climb in an attempt to catch 2000ft. At that time; we heard the flap overspeed warning. I immediately set the flaps to 2 and the PF manually reduced thrust and raised the nose to control the speed. The aircraft slowed below flap overspeed and the PF placed the thrust levers back in the Climb detent. Shortly after that we reached 2000ft; still in a climb. The PF pushed the nose over at 2200ft and began a descent to 2000ft. The aircraft began accelerating again towards a flap overspeed.  I raised the flaps to 1 and the PF once again manually reduced thrust.  Once level at 2000ft and safely below flap overspeed the thrust levers were again placed in the Climb detent; and the autopilot was engaged.The flap overspeed was approximately 5 knots; which was confirmed by maintenance during their inspection.Cause: The causal factors for this event were a combination of Human Error; Automation Management; and Task Saturation.  The initial go around procedures were executed in accordance with SOP; but the maneuver got away from both of us at 1000ft with the radio switch to departure.  The unexpected level-off clearance at 2000ft was the first distraction that took my attention away from aircraft configuration and speed monitoring. It had the same effect on the PF whose full attention went to altitude control.  The high rate of climb and proximity to the new level-off altitude became the primary concern of both of us. So much so that the PF reached for the altitude knob to make sure 2000ft was set in the window as quickly as possible.  There was a moment where we both were reaching for the altitude knob and had to establish whose responsibility it was to set it. This momentarily took us both away from the primary flight instruments at a critical time. The result was that we did not configure the aircraft to flaps to2 on cleanup schedule.This failure to continue to maintain the normal configuration schedule was a direct result of task saturation and monitoring error on my part.  I allowed my attention to be drawn to the altitude issue and away from monitoring the aircraft configuration and aircraft performance.  So; task saturation led to an error in monitoring.Additionally; the PF's decision to hand fly the entire go-around with no autopilot added significantly to the workload for both of us. Not engaging the auto pilot earlier in the maneuver required significant attention to be paid to basic flight control and added to the task saturation.The first recommendation I have is to utilize the autopilot as much as possible during a busy maneuver like a go around. That allows both crew members to focus on aircraft trajectory; performance; and configuration; rather than monitoring basic aircraft control.The other recommendation is for me as PM to establish crew duties immediately as soon as it gets busy like that.  As Captain and PM I should have told the PF; since he was hand flying; to concentrate solely on the primary flight instruments and let me listen to departure; set altitude/speed window parameters; and keep him informed of any necessary changes.  Had I focused his attention back to flying the aircraft immediately; he probably would have caught the overspeed before it happened. I also should have recommended that the autopilot be engaged early in the process.The lesson learned for me is that I need to ensure a very thorough brief on pilot duties and automation management during a go-around.  I also need to take a more involved approach during the maneuver to make sure that the PF is solely concentrating on flying the aircraft and controlling its trajectory; velocity; and configuration.
