Synopsis:
Tower Controller and pilot reported a helicopter cleared for a touch and go hovered over the approach end of the runway causing the pilot to take evasive action which resulted in them leaving the runway surface into the grass.

Narrative:
Aircraft X student solo flight was number 3 following a helicopter in the right downwind to [Runway] XXR. She called the traffic to follow in sight and I cleared her as number 3 to the runway touch and go. Number 2 was Aircraft Y; helicopter; I cleared the Aircraft Y for the touch and go also. I handled other traffic and when I looked up Aircraft Y was hovering over the runway. I told him to take off immediately and he slowly lifted up the student solo pilot Aircraft X was crossing the threshold touching down but chose to veer off to the right in the grass. I would reiterate to Aircraft Y; the helo; what a touch and go details and put emphasis on not holding on the runway like a stop and go. I would also make sure Aircraft X doesn't start her base turn so tight that it would be an issue; there was no one behind them this pass. I would also place more of a focus on the student solo Aircraft X to help guide them in a busier pattern like that.

Secondary Narrative:
I am a student pilot training in my personal aircraft; and was flying my second solo in the pattern. On my 5th landing; on downwind; I was advised by Tower that I was number 3 behind a Diamond and a helicopter; cleared touch-and-go for Runway XXR. I repeated instruction and advised that I was looking for traffic. At first; I saw only the Diamond and not the helicopter. While still downwind; I saw the helicopter and advised Tower 'traffic in sight' and continued my downwind some distance behind it before making my base turn. Once I turned base; I was not conscientious about keeping the helicopter in sight. I made the assumption that since I was cleared for [Runway] XXR behind it; and since the Tower had also only cleared the helicopter for touch-and-go; my spacing was adequate. On approach; I was focused on my aiming point and keeping it appropriately 'still' in my windscreen; and did not see the helicopter anywhere near or on the runway. I assumed it had done its touch-and-go and moved on and was not a factor. As I moved my eyes from the aiming point down the runway to the point of starting my flare for landing; I looked straight ahead and suddenly saw the silhouette of the helicopter against the sky; about 30 feet vertical and stopped over [Runway] XXR. I was approaching it very quickly. It was about 2000 feet from the approach end of runway and I realized I could not apply power to do a go-around even if banking I wouldn't have had enough power to clear the helicopter. My split-second instinct was to stay on the ground and try to stop prior to its position. I applied brakes but was not able to come to a stop prior to the helicopter's position so I applied right rudder and steered off of [Runway] XXR into the grass to the right of the runway. When I came to a stop; the helicopter was still hovering in its position over [Runway] XXR; now directly to my left. The helicopter made a call to Tower that it was 'on the move' and then immediately reported that an aircraft had gone off of the runway onto the grass. With the engine still running and seemingly no damage; I called Tower to let them know I was off of [Runway] XXR in the grass and requested assistance. Tower advised the helicopter that they were cleared for touch-and-go only; and they advised that a touch-and-go is what they were doing. Tower asked if I was injured or if there was damage to the aircraft. I advised that I was fine; and said I needed to shut down and inspect my front end to know if there was damage. Tower advised that help was on the way. I then shut down my avionics and all other systems normally; exited my aircraft and found no damage as I inspected it. To avoid the situation occurring in future; PIC should maintain aircraft in sight and declare to ATC if traffic is lost. Additionally; extra training has been advised internally at the FBO to ensure that all students are briefed on helicopter operations and their different approach to a touch and goes; whereby they have to momentarily stop. Should traffic be lost; this should be communicated to ATC and their instructions followed; if a go around is deemed a safe option this could also be performed. Finally; ADS-B traffic should be utilized where available.
