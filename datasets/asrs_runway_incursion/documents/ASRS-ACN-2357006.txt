Synopsis:
Flight Instructor with student reported a critical ground conflict while landing.

Narrative:
ZZZ is turning into a very hazardous operation. The amount of airline enplanements and new foreign student starts has made a very difficult airport that was already overloaded with competing traffic a complete nightmare.Although I respect the controllers wish to serve as many planes as possible; providing traffic separation services outside of the Class D and using shortened non-standard radio phraseology is making matters worse. There will be legal consequences for controllers making pilots outside of the zone follow IFR aircraft with the help of vectors and other references if it ends in a collision. This is not the purpose of a Class D facility which is to serve the landing and take-off separation inside of Class D Airspace. It has also created a distraction for the tower controller today.We were on a short final to Runway XXR and told to expect a late landing clearance. Subsequently another Piper Cherokee Warrior was cleared to 'line up and wait'. At approximately 300 feet my student performed a go-around and offset to the right. The tower gave us instructions to commence a right downwind at 500 feet after clearing the tower safely and we were then told to make a three mile final after confirming that we had a landing aircraft in sight. The tower then sarcastically chastised us for believing that the final was closer to two miles. I then asked him to confirm landing clearance and was again belittled for him believing that the clearance had already been given.We landed uneventfully and exited at taxiway 1.I called ground frequency multiple times without response. Believing it safer to move the aircraft then occupy an exit point of the runway; we made a right turn on Taxiway 2 and exited the first left turn onto the ramp; the total distance being about 150 yards.The voice had changed from a male to a female voice on the frequency and no notification was made of a controller-change over the frequency prior to this.There's a lot to unravel here; and it pains me to have to report the inadequacies of this facility. First of all; I am a current large transport aircraft pilot with decades of experience; many type ratings; licensed in several different countries. As a former Chief Pilot and Check Airman and former Chief Flight instructor; I have never seen anything so dangerous; unprofessional and poorly run as the facility at ZZZ; nor have I seen such a wrong allocation of Class D Airspace when quite clearly; Class C Airspace as a bare minimum should be applied.There is no flow for departures and arrivals by sector; no reporting points; no entry or exit altitudes. This is unheard of in any other high volume training airspace anywhere else in the world.Quite clearly; a midair collision is imminent.
