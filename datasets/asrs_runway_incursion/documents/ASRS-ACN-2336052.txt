Synopsis:
General aviation instructor reported a near miss with another aircraft; when the other aircraft entered the runway and departed; while the instructor's aircraft was on final approach at MKT; a non-towered airport.

Narrative:
On Day 0 at approximately XA:30; IP Person A and student Person B were inbound to land at Mankato (MKT) from a flight lesson in ZZZ; flying in Aircraft X. Runway 33 was the active; and we had entered the pattern via a midfield cross. Flying the pattern was uneventful until the final leg.As we turned on final; my student made a radio call stating our position. We had traffic in sight (Aircraft Y) that was holding short of Runway 33. It was a calm and clear day so our groundspeed was probably around 70-80 kts. We heard Aircraft Y make a call stating that they were departing on Runway 33. In my mind I thought 'Wow that's cutting it close but they could probably make it'; but then; I didn't see the aircraft roll across the hold short line after making their departure call. I then thought 'Maybe they just now saw us on final and are waiting for us to land'; but I still maintained vigilance that they might depart anyways. Right after I had that thought; they rolled past the threshold line onto the runway for takeoff. By that time we were less than a mile on final. I had Person B stay on the controls and fly the plane while I watched the traffic outside. We saw them start their roll; but determined we would not be able to land safely without creating an incursion; so we executed a go around. We side-stepped to ther right to avoid catching up to or overflying the departure traffic; but could only side-step so much as there was other traffic on the upwind leg of the pattern (Mankato has an upwind leg for traffic sequencing reasons). At one point we were only separated by 100 feet; and I could no longer maintain visual contact with Aircraft Y due to our nose-up attitude for the go around and our spacing being so close. I'm not sure what our altitude was when we executed the go-around; as I was keeping my eyes outside watching for traffic.As soon as it was safe to do so; my student and I opted to break out of the pattern rather than turning crossdwind in order to recollect and re-assess the situation. When we landed; we each submitted a safety report. We also discussed the situation and determined that we had the right of way. We agreed that something we could have done better was make the go around decision sooner; which would have provided better spacing.
