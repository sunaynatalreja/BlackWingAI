Synopsis:
Air taxi pilot reported the markings and signage at Taxiway Delta and Runway 30/12 at TUS are confusing and can lead to runway and taxiway incursions.

Narrative:
I was cleared for a straight in landing on runway 22 due to approaching the airport from the east. The active runway at the time for other arrivals was runway 12. I have flown to TUS a lot in the past. Normally after touchdown tower will instruct me to do a 180 on the runway and back taxi to alpha and contact ground / proceed to the FBO. Or sometimes I will be instructed to make a left on delta 3 and contact ground. In this case the controller was busy transmitting to another aircraft by the time I had slowed down so I made the first available exit which happened to be taxiway alpha 2. After vacating onto alpha 2 I realized I was now directly in the approach path / area of runway 12 with Aircraft Y on a few mile final for this very runway. I decided to hang another left and get clear of the area on taxiway delta to prevent a problem before contacting tower who then handed me off to ground. The ground controller did not mention anything about an incursion and instructed me to taxi along delta to the ramp as usual.The situation still felt weird to me after the fact however because while I taxied on delta to the ramp I crossed over a red marking on the pavement that said 30-12" that would imply I was just on an active runway without a clearance even though the taxiway turnoff that brought me here didn't say anything. After looking through other safety reports I am not the first person to get confused by the taxiway layout at this specific intersection despite having lots of experience operating here. When you are rolling out on runway 22 taxiway alpha 2 looks like a completely normal turn off. No special markings on the runway and no red signs for an intersecting runway approaching. Alpha 2 is also depicted like any other taxiway on the airport diagram on initial glance. Once you are on alpha 2 however you are on runway 12 and that becomes apparent when you vacate runway 22 and now see runway 12 right in front of you. When you then make either a left or right onto delta you cross over a pair of hold bars that read in red letters on the concrete "30-12" again implying you are / were on a runway even though the taxi diagram depicts it as a taxiway and there are yellow stripe markings on the ground. Upon review after the situation it appears this is known as a "aligned taxiway" which the FAA has since prohibited being used for new construction or reconstruction of taxiways presumably because it confuses pilots. There is currently a hot spot on the airport diagram for one side of the intersection however it does not mention the above scenario but instead only acknowledges that pilots tend to cross the hold markings when taxiing to runway 12 via delta presumably due to the uncommon layout of the intersection.I believe that something design wise visually needs to change at this intersection to prevent future issues and needless pilot deviations as in the case of some other safety reports. Pilots are misinterpreting the existing markings and signage and the airport diagram doesn't add much clarity to the situation especially when the hot spot marking is only on one side of the 3 possible incursion points. In the meantime the best remedy is a reminder to be very vigilant when taxiing near runway intersections and always making sure to have the airport diagram pulled up on an EFB or printed out on paper however in this case that did not help me since my immediate focus was on safely vacating the runway I had just landed on."
