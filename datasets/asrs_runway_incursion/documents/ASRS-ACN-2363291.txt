Synopsis:
General aviation pilot crew reported a near collision on the takeoff roll with an aircraft taking off on the crossing runway at a non-towered airport. The Captain delayed the rotation to avoid the other aircraft.

Narrative:
While operating a passenger flight from ZZZ to ZZZ1 we planned a departure on RW XX with a right forward quartering headwind /crosswind. We taxied to runway XX and announced our intentions on the Unicom frequency. We received our IFR clearance on the ground via ZZZ CTR on the radio as well.We heard no other traffic on the Unicom frequency of XXX.XX We saw no visible traffic during our taxi; in the air or on the ground. The Cessna 182 had taxied much earlier than us so assumed he had departed on his flight to ZZZ2. There were no radio transmissions from the Cessna 182 and that was confirmed via the FBO audio recorded during that time period.Our transmissions were on the recording stating our intentions to taxi to RW XX. We completed our taxi check and were holding short of RW XX. The Co-pilot transmitted our departure intentions for ZZZ RW XX and we began our normal takeoff roll. The aircraft accelerated normally and the Co-Pilot called V1 and Rotate; At that moment the C182 appeared at about 11 o clock in the windshield and approximately 50 to 75 ft. in the air. It was too late for an aborted takeoff as it appeared we would pass barely underneath the the C182. A rotation at that instant would have caused impact with the C182. I felt the safest option was to delay the rotation and remain on the runway for a couple of seconds as the traffic passed over us. It happened very quickly and we rotated and resumed the climb away from the runway. The remainder of the flight was normal and uneventful. The incident occurred due to lack of situational awareness resulting from a lack of radio communication. I was unaware visually of another aircraft positioned at RW XY prior to beginning the takeoff roll. This incident could have been avoided by pilots verifying correct Unicom/ CTAF frequencies in use at uncontrolled airports. Visually verifying other aircraft when possible and the use of all available appropriate aircraft lighting to enhance visibility.

Secondary Narrative:
Our aircraft was departing on runway XX. I made CTAF calls taxiing out of the FBO via 1 and made a call once on runway XX ready for takeoff. Another aircraft was seen taxiing on the other side of the airfield via taxiway 2. No radio calls were heard from that aircraft. Visual contact was lost as it blended in to the airport environment at the approach end of runway XY. We started our takeoff roll and as we passed V1 speeds we visually saw the other aircraft departing runway XY. It was approximately 75-100 ft. AGL as it passed directly over us at the intersection of Runway XX and Runway XY. We elected to stay on the ground/runway at takeoff speed until the overflying aircraft was not a factor overhead. We then executed our takeoff with sufficient runway left to safely get airborne.Once airborne at approximately 1000 feet AGL I made another call on CTAF that we were turning northbound and departing the airport environment. No response was heard from any other aircraft in the area; including the aircraft we narrowly missed.Once landed at our destination in ZZZ1 we called airport personnel via cell phone to determine if they could hear our calls on the radio. They returned our call the next morning and stated that they heard ALL of our radio calls but none from the other aircraft. We had briefly talked with the other aircraft's pilot/occupant and knew that they were heading to ZZZ2. We called company at ZZZ2 and determined the aircraft's identification to be Aircraft Y. We did not make any contact with the crew of Aircraft Y as they had left the airfield.We believe that Aircraft Y may have been on the wrong CTAF frequency in ZZZ which is why we did not hear any radio transmissions from them nor did airport personnel. If true this directly caused the problem. To prevent this from happening again; we should have attempted to make direct radio contact with the other aircraft we saw taxiing either on CTAF or Guard frequency. If no contact made; we should have delayed taking our runway until we could absolutely confirm that other aircraft had taken off from their runway and was no longer a hazard.
