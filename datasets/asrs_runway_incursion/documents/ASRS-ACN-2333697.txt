Synopsis:
B737 Captain reported they continued their takeoff roll after Takeoff Hold Lights illuminated and they observed an aircraft on short final for the intersecting runway.

Narrative:
I was the pilot monitoring while the FO performed the take off from ZZZ XXL.  We were aware of the 787 on final to XXR as the controller had made reference to it in the take off clearance.  We were above 80 kts call as the last lights of the Runway Status Lights/Take Off Hold system illuminated just as the last light elements disappeared under the nose.  I recall seeing 96 knots in the HUD (Heads up Display) as we were well into the takeoff roll just past 1 intersection.  I looked and saw the 787 was on very short final; looked ahead and could not see any other threats entering the runway and decided it was best to continue. As we flew through the intersection the tower was already telling the  787 to go around; I again looked right and could see the 787 was in the flare and low enough that it was causing mist and water to displace out from the jet blast and wing downwash. They appeared to be just short of 2 intersection on XXR.  We continued our departure with no other issue or anything being said by ATC.The illumination of the lights so late in the take off caused me some confusion as to perform an RTO (Rejected Takeoff) or continue.  I quickly was thinking it is not a condition we brief to reject for above 80 kts; and if I did reject it might put us stopped right in the intersection of the runways possibly in the path of the 787.  Continuing seemed best but what if the lights were for a threat of another vehicle or aircraft we did not see yet and not the 787.  In the end I believe it was the best decision given the circumstances but it brings up some deficiency in our awareness and reaction to these lights. Had the lights illuminated earlier with more in view; I would have been more inclined to stop. However; only seeing the last lights illuminate in a split second as they disappeared from view created great uncertainty.  I tried searching our company documents for reference to these lights and could only find one article from an report; Day 0that discussed a similar event with a crew out of ZZZ1.  This leaves us with the guidance that is found in the AIM as our only reference.  Inclusion of the THL lights in the take off rejection considerations section of the AOM/FOM and training would be appropriate.
