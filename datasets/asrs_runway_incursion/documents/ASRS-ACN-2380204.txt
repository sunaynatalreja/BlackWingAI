Synopsis:
General aviation pilot reported landing without clearance at COE as the sectional charts do not indicate there is a tower and suggested this information be announced in the AWOS.

Narrative:
On Day 1; I was exposed to a disturbing circumstance that I feel should be corrected for the benefit of pilots and safety. My cross-country journey began at ZZZ in the morning of Day 0. I had a passenger; an experienced cross-country land and sea pilot as well as a photographer. We were loaded with gear for our journey to ZZZ1. To stay within weight requirements; we were limited on fuel capacity which required us to make several short flights from airport to airport for fuel stops.We stayed overnight at ZZZ2 that night after taking a tour of the lake for photos. The next morning; we toured another lake for more photos. As we continued our journey to ZZZ1; we took the opportunity for more photos of rivers and lakes on the way. More fuel stops were made along the path.Arriving at Coeur d'Alene Lake; we toured for more photos and noticed the Sea Plane Base (S76) had two planes and a ferry boat docked and there was no room for another plane. We decided it would be best to fuel at COE; a non-towered airport shown on the current Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) sectional chart.After checking the AWOS at COE channel 135.075 for wind and other information; it favored Runway 20 for landing. With no additional comments; I switched to the UNICOM channel 122.8 as indicated on the sectional chart and announced my intentions five to six miles out that I was approaching and planned to overfly the airport 500 feet above the pattern altitude to verify the wind to Runway 20.During my approach; five miles out to the space COE; I did notice one aircraft departing to the west. Otherwise; there was no other traffic in the area after checking the windsock and verifying that Runway 20 was best wind for land. Also noticed there was no traffic in the air or on the ground. I announced that I was maneuvering for a mid-field crossing to enter left downwind; for Runway 20. After turning downwind; I made another radio call stating I was on an extended downwind for Runway 20. I lowered my landing gear and flaps; then announced I left base for Runway 20. I established a stabilized approach and announced final for Runway 20.Immediately after touching down; I heard a radio call about switching to 120 something? I exited the runway at the first taxi exit and headed for what I thought was the fuel area as there were Phillips 66 signs. Then I got a radio call asking my intentions. I responded that I needed some fuel. The controller told me that it was a towered airport now and I needed to switch to 120.125 frequency. I told him I had no idea as the current FAA chart indicates a non-towered airport with fuel. He said it was on the NOTAM and I made a mistake. He instructed me to taxi down to the run-up area of Runway 02 for further instructions. He told me that this has been a common miscommunication with pilots for a while.After fueling up for our last leg to ZZZ1; I called ATC for progressive taxi instructions to the tower for better explanation. The tower was a bit difficult to see or find as it was a makeshift setup of container boxes stacked up. After climbing to the top of containers where the controllers were; we had a nice cordial discussion regarding the lack of information available to incoming pilots. Not only is it not on the currently published sectional FAA charts; but it's also not on the NOTAM. The simple solution is to have it announced in the AWOS recording until the charts are updated. Additionally; the UNICOM channel should be monitored by ATC to give pilots the correct frequency as they enter the COE airspace and do not wait until after they have landed.Furthermore; I hope that your organization can be of some help to me in this particular case. As I take aviation responsibilities very seriously and don't want this to be a blemish on my record. I am not sure what I could have done differently except going to a different airport.In conclusion; I hope for the safety of all and the fairness of other pilots; the continuation ofthis problem at COE must be corrected; regardless of their growing pains.
