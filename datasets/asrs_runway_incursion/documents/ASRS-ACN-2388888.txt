Synopsis:
BZN Tower Controller reported BZN Airport Operations has repeatedly published runway closure NOTAMs with inaccurate times. Reporter stated Operations also did not shut off the runway lights to indicate a runway was closed; leading to an aircraft nearly taking off the closed runway as the NOTAM had incorrectly stated it was still open.

Narrative:
I was the Control Tower Operator on duty for the evening shift. I ceased ATC operations at XA:30 and remained in the tower cab completing post-duty administrative tasks. I had been aware of the following NOTAM closing Runway 12/30 1 hour after the tower closed. No other runway closure NOTAM was ever issued concerning 12/30. While on the computer; I heard 2 transmissions on the CTAF. Both at/around XA:35. First; Aircraft X announcing they were taxiing out to Runway 30 for departure. And then a BZN Airport Ops person announcing that Runway 12/30; Runway 3/21; and a long list of taxiways were all closed. No response was heard from Aircraft X and they continued taxiing for departure; indicating they did not hear the advisory of the closures. Despite BZN Airport Operations having the ability to easily shut off and/or lock-out" runway/taxiway lights from the lighting vault; the runway lights for 12/30 remained on; in pilot control mode. The taxiway lights for the closed taxiways remained on too.BZN Airport Operations has repeatedly conducted the unsafe practice of purposely misrepresenting the time on a runway closure NOTAM by over an hour. This is not the first time this has occurred. Their intention almost always is to close 12/30 "after the arrival of the last scheduled air-carrier." However; since air carrier arrival times vary and since there is not a legitimate way to write a NOTAM saying that; they have 2 legitimate options. Either A: Issue a Prior Permission Required (PPR) style NOTAM; which they don't ever do because they are lazy and hate having to deal with phone calls/questions from flight crews and dispatchers coordinating PPRs. Or B: Wait until the last air carrier lands and then issue a standard runway closure NOTAM beginning at that time; with no advanced warning provided to pilots. They choose neither of those legitimate options and rather choose a shady middle ground where they issue a runway closure NOTAM hours or days in advance; but with the start time purposely set later than they actually intend on closing the runway. This gives flight crews known-false information; no advance notice regarding the real start time of the closure; and sets the flight crews up for failure. If flight crews are lucky; BZN Airport Operations waits until the last air carrier arrives; then cancels and reissues the NOTAM with the real start time and then calls Salt Lake Center informing them that they runway is closed time-now. That NOTAM reissuance did not happen tonight however. Per the NOTAMs; Runway 12/30 technically remained open during the incident.BZN Airport Operations has also repeatedly conducted the unsafe practice of failing/refusing to shut off the runway lights to a runway they just closed. Tonight was not the first instance of that either. I understand that the rule forbidding lighting a closed runway (JO7110.65BB 3-4-10-E) only applies to the control tower and does not restrict airport operations from doing so when the tower is closed; but continuing to light a closed runway when you don't have to is just stupid. It's a massive safety risk and one that was entirely unnecessary tonight. Had the runway lights for 12/30 been shut off; I am confident Aircraft X would not have attempted departure on that runway.Suggestions: 1. Issue PPR-style runway closure NOTAMS with the real/actual start time of the closure. Provide sufficient advance notice to aircrews. Grant PPRs as you see fit. 2. Shut off runway lights when closing a runway; if those lights aren't necessary for things like snowplowing or lighting maintenance. 3. FAA should codify (perhaps in an Advisory Circular (AC?)) suggestion #2 as it relates to airport operators when the ATCT is closed for the night."
