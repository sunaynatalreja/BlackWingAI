Synopsis:
General aviation pilot reported LHW has unlit signs that are improperly located and the pavement markings are faded.

Narrative:
On Day 0; I taxied to take off from Runway 33 LHW in Georgia. The hold short line is extremely faded; and is several hundred feet from the beginning of the runway. I overstepped it by about an airplane length. I stopped; called the Tower and apologized. The Tower cleared me for takeoff and instructed me to turn right to a heading less than one mile after Departure End of Runway (DER). I was distracted by the passing of the line; the non-standard takeoff procedure; but believed all was OK.Immediately after takeoff; when I looked at the engine parameters screen; I noticed something was unusual; but it was not immediately clear what that was - all indicators were in the green; but something seemed different than my usual picture. I was already in the air; committed to completing the takeoff; and under the instructions of very short turn clearance. I continued the climb and planned on identifying the issue and dealing with it once stabilized on the climb; after the turn; however; at that point I turned left instead of right. The Tower directed my attention to the issue but gracefully said that there was no need to correct.The irregularity turned out to be higher than normal oil temperature indication. Once that was clear; I performed a precautionary landing. It was an incorrect indication problem.Lessons and conclusion: The initial hold short line crossing was irregular and got me a bit unsettled; and worried about consequences. In conjunction with the irregular indication; I made the second mistake. The conclusion is that I must make sure that I am 100% back to myself before accepting a clearance.Another lesson is that the markings (throughout the field) in LHW are truly bad; and in my mind at an unacceptable level. Signs are unlit; pavement markings are very faded; and the location of signs is improper. I would like to suggest that placing hold-short line locations on taxi diagrams is a good idea; especially when the hold-short line is so far from the actual runway (I estimate at least 250 ft.). I think that the average pilot will look for a hold-short line much closer to the runway and when the line is so faded will easily miss it. This however clearly does not release me from missing it.
