Synopsis:
General aviation instructor pilot reported a traffic coordination conflict while back taxiing on a runway at a non-towered airport with another aircraft on final approach. The aircraft on final performed a go-around to provide separation.

Narrative:
We were conducting stop and gos; but did not have enough runway to take off; so we got clear of the runway because Aircraft Y was on final (we had been coordinating with them the entire time in the traffic pattern). As we entered the runway to begin our back taxi; Aircraft Y made a call on CTAF that an aircraft (Aircraft Z) was on final. Aircraft Z had not made a single radio call on the D56 CTAF; which we had noticed and Aircraft Y confirmed that Aircraft Z had not made a single one. We didn't see them turn base to final and when we heard Aircraft Y make that call and then saw Aircraft Z on final; we swiftly exited the runway. We were off of the runway by the time Aircraft Z would've touched down; but Aircraft Z executed a go-around out of precaution.Cause:  Lack of coordination from Aircraft Z; and I didn't zoom out on the MFD (Multi-function Flight Display) because I still had it zoomed in to determine if we had 2000ft to take off. It was a fault on both ends. Certain fatigue on my end too since this was a late afternoon flight and the second consecutive day where I had an early morning flight and a late afternoon flight in the same day.Suggestions:  All aircraft in the traffic pattern should be coordinating with each other. Also checking the MFD before entering a runway; because traffic might not be talking. I was scanning final as Aircraft Y was coming in to land but at the time; Aircraft Z was likely on base which I didn't see.
