Synopsis:
PA28-181 instructor reported succeeding jet traffic landed prior to instructor exiting runway resulted in a critical ground conflict and runway incursion.

Narrative:
I was providing flight instruction to a private pilot student and conducting takeoffs and landings in the traffic pattern for Runway XX at ZZZ. A student solo aircraft was ahead of us in the pattern; and we elected to extend our downwind leg to allow additional time for that aircraft to clear the runway. We announced our intention to extend downwind on the CTAF.After extending downwind; we turned base and then final. We were established on approximately a 2-mile final when we heard another aircraft report being on a 5-mile final for the same runway. This was the first radio transmission we heard from that aircraft. Prior to turning final; we visually cleared the approach path and did not observe the traffic.I transmitted that we were on a 2-mile final and asked the other aircraft if they had us in sight. The pilot responded that they did. Based on that information; my student and I continued the approach and landing.After touchdown; while we were decelerating and preparing to exit the runway; the other aircraft reported being on short final. We had not yet fully cleared the runway but were in the process of turning off at the next available taxiway. As we initiated the turn; I observed the aircraft; a Cessna Citation; touching down behind us. I took the controls; applied power as necessary; and expedited our runway exit.After landing; the jet crew transmitted that they were experiencing brake issues and requested assistance from the FBO; including a tow.Contributing factors may have included limited situational awareness between aircraft operating in the traffic pattern and the inbound jet. The first transmission we heard from the jet was their 5-mile final call; which limited our awareness of their arrival and closure rate. Additionally; the use of only a tail number rather than a more descriptive aircraft identification made it more difficult to recognize the aircraft type and anticipate its higher approach speed.
