Synopsis:
A helicopter pilot reported they were hovering near the intersection of two runways when an arriving aircraft landed and rolled past them.

Narrative:
While conducting Flight Instruction in a helicopter (CFI positioned left seat; Pilot-Student Right Seat) at the local high traffic-use un-towered airport; we had conducted our approach to the predominant wind use runway XX; making the CTAF radio report of the approach; and runway XX mid-field intersection termination point.    The mid-point area of the runways intersect; with Runways XX and XY crossing; with the winds indicated by windsock on the airfield of 350-010°; 10 knots with occasional gusts during our traffic pattern training.  The runway alignment angle of Runways XX and XY does not allow a direct view on final for Runway XY if positioned near the intersection or north on Runway XX for reference.  This positioning makes it necessary to verify conflicting approach path is clear of course; if directly holding short (responsibly announced) or prior to an initial departure intention from runway XX; neither of which was the case in this situation.  Upon completing our approach to the mid-field intersection on Runway XX; I was allowing the student pilot to stabilize their hover in the wind prior to our take-off checks and prepare for next departure / right traffic pattern.  I heard an aircraft in-bound over the CTAF; making a call with the intent to use Runway XY; despite the winds and traffic pattern in use already; so as to avoid back-taxing to parking; as only Runway XY has a parallel taxiway directly accessible easily to & from the apron / ramp area.  I made another CTAF call stating we were a momentary delay; midfield intersection RWY XX.   I did not hear another CTAF call; however just a few moments later the American A-5 Tiger landed in front on us on Runway XY; (from my right; angled and blocked by the Pilot-Student / Door) rolling directly by within 50' or less from our hovering position on Runway XX; just south of the intersection.   The pilot while; rolling out after landing; proceeded to chastise us over CTAF; stating we 'were too close to the runway and shouldn't be there'.  I restated we literally had called our position on our approach and again just prior to his approach; stating we were still occupying the predominant wind runway at the intersection; using the crossing runway XX as established by us and other aircraft in the recent traffic pattern operations.    The other pilot continued to berate us over the radio; saying we should have moved 'off to the side' since he 'was landing'; insisting that despite the crossing runway in use; the runway stated as occupied and he saw us in position on runway (XX); implied it was his right of way to continue his approach regardless  apparently in lieu of considering a CTAF call about our holding position or the option to go-around since the runway area was already occupied.  There was no inquiry about if we were holding short on Runway XY; or if we could reposition away from the crossing runway (which we can & will do routinely); nor any attempt to abort his landing approach; despite knowing we were still there.   This continues to pose an hazard to flight operations at the airport; where airplane traffic will force repeated runway changes during established traffic pattern operations; in order to simplify their ground taxi to parking.  The airplane traffic continues to demonstrate no intention of clearing final approach or the runway environment on multiple occasions; insisting their landing gives them the right of way to an occupied runway; regardless of any aircraft already present. This has developed into a stigma; instead of addressing the core safety issues of communication; runway / pattern operations in use; traffic flow courtesy and clarifying intentions or going around versus forcing a situation into a Near Mid-Air Collision or Runway Incursion to a known occupied runway.
