Synopsis:
Cessna 172 and C150 pilots reported the C150 pilot over flew the C172 which was still on the runway.

Narrative:
My student pilot solo was back taxiing on runway XX at ZZZ. The taxiway does not extend to the end of the runway so approximately 300-500 feet of back taxiing is required. He began his back taxi to the beginning of the runway when an airplane called out a one mile final call with no prior radio calls. My student pilot had already began the back taxi and did not have an area to exit the runway. He made a radio call to the landing airplane that he had already began the back taxi. They both acknowledged that they had each other in sight. The landing airplane on final continued the approach and landed approximately 2000 feet down the runway. The landing airplane was approximately 150-200 feet above the airplane back taxiing down the runway.

Secondary Narrative:
While making a VOR RNW XX approach to ZZZ I announced my position several times on final approach.  I saw an airplane in what I thought was the runup area of runway XX at a 90 degree angle to the runway. It turned out that that was the turnaround zone he was on and he complained as I overflew that he was on the runway. I went around  to give him the runway. I suggest that all the turn around areas be enlarged with hold short lines.  That would eliminate aircraft occupying the runway before they are ready to takeoff as well as any confusion as to weather one is in a turn around or runup area.  From the air on approach it is difficult to tell whether it is a turn around or runup area.  If an aircraft is in the turn around area for an extended time that suggests the false impression that he is in a runup area and not ready to take off.  Especially if he maintains radio silence as I approach the runway of final.
