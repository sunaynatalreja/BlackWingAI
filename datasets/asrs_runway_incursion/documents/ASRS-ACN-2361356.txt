Synopsis:
Light aircraft pilot reported a non-communicating aircraft entered the opposite end of the runway while landing; resulting in a critical ground conflict.

Narrative:
At just past XA:00 on Day 0 I was returning from ZZZ to LOM and requested RNAV 6 from PHL Approach Control (123.8) for practicing my IFR flow (the field was visual). A request that was accepted by PHL Approach. I was directed to the LASBE (IAF-RNAV 6) and cleared for the approach. On turning to the final approach course; I told PHL approach that I had visual of the field. They advised me that there was no traffic observed. At this time; I canceled my IFR. I switched to CTAF and made a 9-mile final call on the CTAF; followed by a 5-mile final call; followed by a short final call. I did a quick scan of the runway on short final; and everything looked good to land.As I touched down; I see a plane on 24 at the opposite end of the runway; and that is the first time I heard him. His call was to warn me of his presence on the runway. He had just rolled on to 24 and was fortunately stationary at that end. There was no danger of any collision since I stopped within 1500 feet or so of touchdown and taxied off the runway. Once the runway was clear; the other plane departed as soon as I was off the runway. When I walked into the FBO (there is a speaker tuned to CTAF); the people there told me that they heard my 3rd call - the short final call; but not the first two (or did not pay attention). They said they never heard the other plane till he piped up to warn me of his presence when I had already touched down and I was on my landing roll. What did I learn? When I am on a short final; I am generally focused on the touch down point; typically the numbers at LOM; and once I pass the threshold and begin my level out and flare is when I raise my eyes to the end of the runway. I think that is what I did on this landing as well and by the time the presence of the plane at the other end registered with me; I was on the ground and slowing down. It was too late to go around. I should not assume that the runway is mine even if I have been making calls; scanning and hearing nothing on the radio. I should always be more watchful and continue scanning the entire runway environment; including at this late stage in my landings.
