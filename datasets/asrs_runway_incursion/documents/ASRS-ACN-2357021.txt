Synopsis:
General aviation pilot reported a critical ground conflict during approach with an aircraft landing opposite direction runway.

Narrative:
I was on short final at ZZZ for runway XX. It was sunset. The entire runway was in shadow. The sun was in my eyes from the sunset at my right. I had repeatedly tried to activate the PAPI using 5 clicks on the radio; but it was not activating and was discussing with my pilot/passenger in the right seat why it was not activating. I did not realize that I was on the wrong frequency and not on ZZZ CTAF. I made a call that I was on final runway XX at ZZZ about 3 miles out. I did not see any other aircraft on ADSB north of the field; and I'm not sure I looked south of the field on the map to see if there was anyone on ADSB. The wind was calm; so either runway was a candidate for takeoffs and landings. At about 500 feet AGL/MSL my right-seater and I both noticed at the same time an aircraft's landing lights on the runway. I was doing a short field landing; so was slow; and so I carefully broke off to the left without adding G loading; adding full power; and gently entered a climb with full flaps; gradually working the flaps out. The aircraft must have just landed on runway XY without me hearing them on the radio because I was on the wrong frequency; and without realizing I was on final for XX because they didn't hear my transmission; and they probably didn't notice me on ADSB; if they even have it. Once I was in a stable climb; I realized I wasn't on frequency and changed to CTAF and heard the other pilot calling me. I replied explaining it was entirely my fault because I had been on the wrong frequency; and the other pilot was gracious about the situation. I'm assuming the other plane must have taxied off the runway and to the ramp because I didn't see them after that. We set up for another landing; which was uneventful.The problem was that I don't have a correct-frequency check as a checklist item; and skipped verifying that I was on the correct CTAF. This was compounded by the runway being entirely in shadow and the sun in my eyes. The PAPI not turning on should have alerted me to the fact that I was on the wrong frequency. The short field landing I was doing also had me in a nose-high attitude behind the power curve; which made it slightly harder to see down the runway. I was also focused on maintaining the plane at a low airspeed; lined up for landing; during the slow approach; which is a bit more demanding and may have been an additional distraction from the issue unfolding. The plane that had just landed on XX may have turned on their landing lights on the runway to alert me of their presence; because neither my right seat pilot/passenger or I noticed them until the plane appeared halfway down the runway. To avoid this in future I plan to add a frequency check to my checklists; make sure I run my checklists every time; make sure my landing lights are on for takeoffs and landings; and consider what else may be wrong when I notice something 'weird' like a PAPI not activating.
