Synopsis:
General aviation pilot reported two near misses at W28; a non-towered airport.

Narrative:
On Day 0 starting at approximately XA:50 local; two near-collision events occurred within a short amount of time at Sequim Valley Airport (W28); involving myself (Aircraft X); an Aircraft Y; and Aircraft Z.Incident 1 -- Runway Incursion by Aircraft Y Causing Me to Abort Takeoff.(The conflict data entered above on this form refers to this specific incident. The 2nd incident described below; concerns a 3rd plane; has even smaller margins for their near-miss.)I was operating Aircraft X and had completed all pre-departure checks. I made radio calls on CTAF 122.7 announcing my intention to taxi onto and depart Runway 09. After lining up at the Runway 09 threshold; I observed Aircraft Y move from the parking area to the runway edge at midfield (2100' ahead from me); then taxi onto the runway and proceed toward Runway 27 -- the opposite end from where I was positioned. No radio calls were made by the aircraft at any point during this time. I made multiple calls to the aircraft and multiple radio checks; no response was received. I elected to abort my intended takeoff and vacate the runway rather than risk departure with an aircraft of unknown intent on the runway.After I vacated; the Aircraft Y pilot then disclosed he had been on the wrong frequency the entire time. Despite this; his first action upon returning to the correct frequency was to announce his departure without first assessing the traffic situation or acknowledging the conflict he had caused.Incident 2 -- Near Midair Collision between Aircraft Y and Aircraft Z:At approximately XB:04 local; while Aircraft Y was positioned at the Runway 27 threshold preparing to depart; Aircraft Z executed what appeared to be a go-around or touch-and-go on Runway 09 and climbed out directly over Aircraft Y at an estimated vertical separation of no more than 100 feet. Aircraft Y appeared unaware that Aircraft Z had passed overhead. Aircraft Z subsequently called on CTAF to apologize; disclosing they had also been on the wrong frequency throughout their approach and pattern.Contributing Factors:Two of three aircraft at the airport were simultaneously tuned to the wrong CTAF frequency; rendering the standard self-announce separation system ineffective.W28's runway configuration requires prolonged runway occupancy to reach thresholds; increasing exposure time and collision risk.The Aircraft Y pilot; after discovering his frequency error; did not pause to assess the traffic situation before proceeding with departure.The narrow runway (40 ft) with no parallel taxiway significantly reduces margins for error.What Prevented a Worse Outcome:I visually detected Aircraft Y before initiating my takeoff roll and held position.I elected to vacate the runway rather than assume the other pilot's intentions.Aircraft Z's go-around (likely triggered by sighting Aircraft Y on the runway) provided vertical separation.Recommended Preventive Measures:Encourage pilots who discover a frequency error to pause; listen; and build situational awareness of the existing traffic situation before taking any further action.At narrow runways like W28 where no parallel taxiway exists; hold-short lines should be positioned to provide greater lateral clearance from the runway edge; and pilots should be explicitly encouraged -- through signage or Chart Supplement notes -- to perform a thorough visual check for aircraft already on the runway before entering.
