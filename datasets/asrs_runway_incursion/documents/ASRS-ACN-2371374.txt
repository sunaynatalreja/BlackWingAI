Synopsis:
Tower Controller reported a firefighting truck and associated vehicles crossed a runway they had been instructed to hold short of at the same time a departing aircraft was taking off.

Narrative:
Our facility is understaffed. We are allowed 12; 14 with training. We have 9; with 1 controller medically disqualified since the start of the busy season. Day 0 is the final day of the busy season. All our controllers are burned out. Everyone has been doing 6 day work weeks; lots of 10 hour days. Additionally in an attempt to get more staffing; our manager has increased our required time on position and reduced break times and lunch breaks. Moral of the story; we are burnt out. I had been fighting a cold or sickness of some sort and am not 100% at this time. I'm tired; I'm sick. I'm now in my 9th or 10th hour of work. The airport had an event on Day 0. There were several aircraft participating. Before the event; the aircraft altogether did about 100 separate ops in the traffic pattern; wearing out my supervisor and I; the only two there that morning. Luckily a third; fresh controller worked the event itself when he arrived to work at XA:15.During the event; all ARFF (Airport Rescue and Firefighting) units staged at Location X; which is across the airfield from the fire station. After the event was over; they called to relocate back to the fire station; needing to cross both parallel runways. My supervisor was working LC; at my request; as I was just so worn out and could barely talk or think anymore; that I felt I would only be safe on ground control; if that. So ARFF calls me and requests to cross both runways back to the fire station. At this time; Aircraft X is lining up to depart rwy XXL. I coordinate a conditional crossing reference the departing aircraft. My SUP approves the conditional.'ARFF and company; CROSS runway XX right at 1. HOLD SHORT runway XX left at 1.'I receive a perfect readback. I watch very carefully as the 3 ARFF units cross XXR. They get to the other side (Taxiway 2) and come to a full stop after clearing the runway. I literally nod my head and say to myself; that's what I want to see. I turn to watch this beautiful aircraft depart XXL and to know when it has passed Taxiway 1 so I can cross the ARFF units. Looks good; roaring down the runway. I make one last check on the ARFF units and turn my head toward them one more time. As my eyes see ARFF; I observe them moving fast (25-40 mph) across TWY 2. I key up the frequency and pretty much yell; 'ARFF; STOP!!!' The lead fire truck slams on it's breaks. I watch as the truck tips forward in its best attempt to stop. The other trucks also stop. ARF is over the hold short line. Seeing that they have all come to a complete stop; I quickly glance over to the aircraft to see if there is any way to cancel take off clearance. It is just airborne between taxiways 3 and 1. At this point; it was safer to let them takeoff. I clutch my chest because I literally can't fathom the thought of what had almost just occurred.The aircraft has now passed and I give the ARFF units clearance to cross runway XXL. I got off position quickly thereafter and left for the day.Recommendation: All of our controllers have had negative experiences with ARFF and how many mistakes on the airfield they have made. I love fire fighters; but these guys (and gals) need more training. The controller's have talked about having them over to the tower and giving them classes during our slow period; but it never seems to happen. Given incidents like this; perhaps the FAA can mandate some driver's tests or something for ARFF on the airfield.Nothing follows.
