Synopsis:
General aviation instructor reported a runway incursion at a non-towered airport when taking off without adequate spacing with an aircraft on final approach. The aircraft on final performed a go-around; which then conflicted with the instructor's aircraft during the climb.

Narrative:
My student and I planned to do a flight where we stayed in the pattern to practice landings. The incident happened at ZZZ; an uncontrolled airfield. On Departure; we lined up on Runway XX at ZZZ behind traffic executing a touch and go (a Cessna 172). A Experimental RV-10 was on final runway XX. I miss-judged the amount of spacing and time that we had to get off of the runway before landing traffic (Experimental RV-10) would be landing. I caused the Experimental RV-10 to go around as we took off. The Experimental RV-10 offset from the runway; and us; by only about 100-200 feet and was below us in altitude for around 10 seconds; they had around 20-30 knots of overtake speed on us; they accelerated ahead of us; then turned in front of us to the left for their left crosswind turn. Meanwhile there is still a Cessna 172 on the departure leg in front of me. The Experimental RV-10 turned left crosswind in front of me and behind the Cessna 172; essentially 'cutting off' the Cessna 172 who was about to turn crosswind in the pattern. The chain of events arose when I; the instructor; allowed our aircraft to take the runway when there was not enough space to takeoff before landing traffic. It was pure human error; and I realized as soon as we pulled onto the runway that there would not be enough space. In the future; I will continue to hold short of the runway if there is any doubt about whether there is enough space/time. Me pulling out and taking the runway when there was not enough time/space and 'cutting off' the RV-10 snowballed into the Experimental RV-10 'cutting off' a different plane in the pattern. Mistakes happen and I was the first to admit that I was at fault. The incident became a larger issue when the Cessna 172 revealed that they were on an FAA checkride. I essentially started the chain of events that led to the 'cutting off' of an airplane doing an FAA checkride with a Designated Pilot Examiner.
