Synopsis:
B737 Captain reported a traffic conflict on final approach with an aircraft on the runway resulting in an ATC directed go-around. The go-around resulted in an undesired aircraft state before regaining aircraft control and a return for another approach and landing.

Narrative:
A complex go-around created by an ATC landing/take-off conflict led to an undesired aircraft state in which the PM perceived safety was in jeopardy and took control of the aircraft.The flight began eventfully with a passenger and service animal issue that caused a delayed departure. Once resolved; the taxi out through initial approach was unremarkable. The workload and complexity began during the latter stages of the arrival and clearance for the approach.The weather was approximately OVERCAST010 to FEW050 and there was a NOTAM for our landing runway (XXR) that advised 'Special Authorization Cat 1 (ILS) procedure Not Authorized; unless advised by ATC that cranes are down.' As we were cleared for the approach and above the glideslope (~2000' AGL); I directed the PM to inquire on the status of the cranes. We were informed multiple times that the 'cranes are up' and there was an unfamiliarity from ATC about this NOTAM; but eventually a declaration that 'we are authorized for the approach.' Meanwhile; I had disconnected the autopilot and autothrottle; accelerating about five knots; but intercepted the glideslope configured gear/F15; slowing from 165kts to target speed at the FAF/1500' AGL. We briefly discussed that we felt safe continuing the approach; completing the landing checklist prior to 1000' AGL and entering IMC. This phase was the first element of complexity during this approach.Upon breaking out of the weather at approximately 800' AGL; I observed an airplane on the runway and queried the PM if he saw the same thing. He confirmed there was an airplane there; followed by ATC providing instructions to it and the preceding aircraft. Next; we verified stable at 500' AGL and were discussing the probability of a go-around as the aircraft was slow to begin the takeoff roll. ATC instructed us to go around at approximately 300' AGL; but with noticeable closure on the impending lift-off of the other aircraft. I initiated the go around in accordance with the SOP; while offsetting to the right (away from the other runway) and remaining under the weather layer; keeping the departing aircraft in sight. I was unable to verify any of the PM go-around actions as I was primarily concerned about flightpath deconfliction with the other aircraft and verbally said 'what does he want us to do' as I estimated our distance to the other aircraft nearing 3000' nose-tail separation. ATC then gave us a left turn to 060 (nearly across the taking off aircraft's flight path) and climb to 3000' MSL; and after a slight delay; I complied with that instruction; beginning the climb back into IMC. This phase was the second element of complexity during this approach.As we climbed into the weather and I transitioned to instruments; I observed the aircraft passing through approximately 1200' MSL at approximately 12 degrees nose high pitch; but with an airspeed of approximately 190 KIAS and decreasing rapidly with the trend arrow approaching 160 KIAS. I immediately lowered the nose towards 5 degrees nose high as I scanned across the other side of the cockpit to analyze the poor aircraft performance. During this time; I believe I said '(check) Flaps' or possibly 'level change; set Climb Speed (CMS); Flaps 5;' but more attention was narrowly focused on determining the throttle setting/engine performance and aircraft gear/flap configuration. I continued to lower the flight path towards level flight; advanced the throttles slightly; and did observe increasing airspeed; as well as a momentary sink rate of approximately 500' FPM. There was comm from both ATC and the PM during this point that I did not fully interpret while focused on precisely controlling the flight path; while also still attempting to diagnose the performance degradation. At this point; without any Enhanced Ground Proximity Warning System (EGPWS) aural warnings; the PM perceived 'red terrain' on the Navigation Display and said 'climb' and then 'I have the aircraft.' Obviously; this was also startling; but I released control of the aircraft and tried to make the mental shift to gain Situational Awareness (SA) as the new PM. I did not immediately notice any 'red terrain'; but did perceive a significant increase in pitch attitude. Still without certainty of the flap position; I begin moving the flaps to a lower setting; neither the FO nor I can definitively say who moved the flaps to 5; though we know he did press LVL Change and set CMS at some point in this immediate sequence of transitioning PF/PM roles. While still gaining SA; I observed 'yellow terrain' on our nose starting at approximately five nautical miles and made a request to ATC that 'we need a turn away.' ATC subsequently provided a frequency change; the new PF also said things during this time that I did not fully comprehend. I observed the aircraft continuing to accelerate; nearing 250 knots and I raised the flaps to UP; the 'FLAPS; FLAPS' aural warning also sounded at some point during this sequence of me functioning as the PM. The PF at some point engaged Command B and we cleared the weather back into VMC. This phase was the third element of complexity during this approach.Now on top of the weather with a stable aircraft and on a good vector and altitude; we were able to restore normal CRM; and executed the after take-off checklist. We double checked each other and the aircraft; returned to the original PF/PM roles; completed a descent check; and uneventful landing and taxi-in (though there was still concern/uncertainty regarding the NOTAMs referencing the cranes; but we had not noticed any on the previous approach).  Due to being late; we only very briefly debriefed in the airplane after completing the parking checklist and I said we would discuss prior to the next flight. The FO left the jet as I stayed to wish passengers farewell and apologize again for the inconvenience. Upon exiting; I saw the CA of the next flight on the jetbridge and we discussed the passenger service animal and NOTAM issue for several minutes. I arrived to the next aircraft and began a deliberate debrief with the FO; recognizing that we will probably be late departing. During our discussions; it became apparent to me that we may have oversped the flaps; I then left the middle of our debrief to go back to the other aircraft to inform the Captain of our suspicion; stating that it was less than 10 knots; for less than 10 seconds; at Flaps 5 or less at ~2000' MSL. I returned to satisfactorily continue our debrief; double checking on the fitness of both the FO and my own fitness for duty for the next flight. He stated that he was; I agreed with his assessment; and did not have concerns regarding my fitness. The next flight was conducted uneventfully.To recap and summarize; the slightly varied and out-of-sync SA among the PF; PM; and ATC resulted in multiple missed/delayed comm and lack of shared mental model on the flight deck (degraded CRM); ultimately producing a poorly executed go around.
